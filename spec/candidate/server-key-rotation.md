# Candidate profile: chained server-key rotation

**Status:** candidate normative for an explicitly selected future rotation profile only. This is not current v0.1, SDK support, freeze, Task 57 acceptance, or a promise that any current client can rotate. Existing static pins remain static unless an application deliberately provisions and opens a separate rotation snapshot. The selected direction permits arbitrarily many successor announcements, including multiple pending announcements with no one-pending or cooldown rule. The timing, codec, API, persistence, status codes, and bounds below are candidate technical defaults, not additional owner approvals.

## 1. Selected schedule and response signing

Let `K0` be the independently trusted initial Ed25519 server key. Each sequential record `R_i`, `i >= 1`, introduces adjacent key `K_i` at fixed logical publication-authorization time `P_i`, an integer whole Unix second. `P_i` is nondecreasing by ordinal. The activation is checked `A_i = P_i + 172800`; reject overflow or any value beyond the current 12-digit Unix timestamp domain. `P_i` is sampled once at the authorized publication intent/authorization linearization and is immutable thereafter. It is not the time a client receives the record, time a page is served, restart time, or a timer reset.

At response signing-time sample `s`, select the key with the greatest ordinal among `K0` and every `K_i` for which `A_i <= s`. Equal activations are resolved by greatest ordinal, so equal-time intermediate keys have an empty response interval. `K0` signs before `A1`; each `K_i` signs in `[A_i,A_(i+1))` once its successor is published; the latest key's interval is unbounded above until a successor is published. For example, if `P1` is Monday 10:00 and `P2` Monday 11:00, `K0` switches to `K1` Wednesday 10:00 and `K1` expires when `K2` takes over Wednesday 11:00. `K1` has no independent 48-hour TTL from its own announcement. No successor delays, cancels, reschedules, or extends a previously fixed activation/cutoff. No product-level limit on pending announcements, one-pending rule, or cooldown is permitted.

A pending tail's private key may authorize its successor before it becomes eligible to sign responses; e.g. `K1` authorizes `R2`, never `K0`. Authorization capability and response-signing eligibility are distinct. Keep required signer handles available for every pending authorization and currently active interval; missing signer readiness fails closed without fallback.

The response `Jack-Auth` tuple's `kid` and `ts` MUST come from the same selected signer and same signing-time sample. Buffer and sign the exact final body once. Immediately before emitting, take a fresh whole-second sample and revalidate latest committed chain, generation/fence, signer readiness, and that the selected key remains eligible at that fresh sample. If the boundary was crossed or state changed, discard the prepared response and fail closed: never switch `kid` or `ts`, re-sign under another key, fallback, or retry. This is an emission authorization linearization check; it cannot revoke bytes already physically returned, nor guarantee a signature remains eligible at last-byte delivery time.

**Publication visibility limitation:** `P_i` is the fixed logical authorization timestamp, not first physical delivery. The authorized chain becomes serviceable only after exact durable acknowledgment, but durable authorization and first physical visibility to every client are not atomic. Therefore this contract does not guarantee a full 48 hours from first physical visibility. A delayed visibility keeps the fixed `P_i/A_i`; it MUST NOT move the timer. This is the explicitly recorded material availability limitation, not an open question for this candidate and not an owner claim of physical-delivery timing.

## 2. Canonical statement codec

All strings used for exact binding are UTF-8 scalar strings with no normalization, case folding, URL reconstruction, redirect substitution, or network-selected values. The server and client use the exact configured endpoint URL and exact configured `aud`; URL must additionally satisfy existing client URL policy. Candidate limits: endpoint 1–2048 UTF-8 bytes, audience 1–1024 UTF-8 bytes, decoded complete record at most 8192 bytes. Reject malformed UTF-8/lone surrogates and over-limit values before expensive verification. Domain `D` is exact ASCII `mcp-jack/server-key-rotation/min1`.

`B_i` is exactly ten ASCII lines joined by LF (U+000A), with no trailing LF, BOM, CR, or whitespace:

```text
mcp-jack/server-key-rotation/min1
<base64url(UTF8(exact_endpoint_url))>
<base64url(UTF8(exact_aud))>
<i>
<previous_complete_record_hash>
<old_kid>
<new_kid>
<new_public_key>
<P_i>
<A_i>
```

`i` is canonical positive decimal 1 through `2^53-1`, no sign/leading zero, checked sequential increment with no wrap. For `R1`, `previous_complete_record_hash` is exactly `-`, anchored only to the OOB `K0`/ordinal-zero checkpoint. Later it is the canonical 43-character unpadded base64url SHA-256 digest of the exact complete preceding `R`, not parsed/re-serialized data. `old_kid`/`new_kid` are existing `kid = base64url(SHA-256(raw_32_byte_public_key))`; each key is exactly 32 bytes, canonical/admitted under [01](../01-authentication.md) and [03 §2](../03-user-management.md), and the `new_kid` is recomputed. `P_i` and `A_i` are canonical unsigned decimal seconds, 0 through 999999999999 (at most 12 digits), and `A_i` MUST equal checked `P_i+172800`. Same-second publication times are allowed; ordinal is the tie-breaker.

Proofs and complete record bytes are:

```text
proof_new = Ed25519_sign(new_private,
    ASCII(D || "/next-proof\n") || B_i)
old_authorization = Ed25519_sign(old_private,
    ASCII(D || "/authorize\n") || B_i || LF || ASCII(base64url(proof_new)))
R_i = B_i || LF || ASCII(base64url(proof_new)) || LF ||
      ASCII(base64url(old_authorization))
```

Thus `R_i` is exactly twelve LF-separated ASCII lines with no trailing LF. Both signatures are canonical unpadded base64url of exactly 64 bytes. Use the existing strict public-key admission and signature rules; do not invent or rely on a new Ed25519 equation/backend behavior. Base64url MUST be canonical unpadded RFC 4648 §5 with zero unused trailing bits and decode/re-encode identity. SHA-256 for chain hashing covers exact complete `R` bytes. The outer ordinary v1 response signature authenticates its exact response body; there is no JSON canonicalization of the record or second body signature.

## 3. `jack.server.key` read-only delivery API

The proposed new protocol-native tool name is exactly **`jack.server.key`** (not the existing user-key tool `jack.key.rotate`). It is read-only and requires ordinary authenticated v1 request processing; its nonce must meet whichever explicitly selected durable-nonce profile the deployment uses. It never reaches the wrapped MCP handler. It is out-of-band/profile selected, not remotely negotiated. The server obtains binding from its exact configured endpoint/audience, not request arguments. The request is a JSON-RPC 2.0 `tools/call` over HTTP POST to the configured endpoint, with `params.name` exactly `jack.server.key` and strict `params.arguments` containing exactly:

```json
{"after_ordinal": 0, "after_hash": "-"}
```

The candidate parser MUST inspect the raw argument-object member sequence before any last-occurrence-wins object decoder and reject duplicate member names; it does not change parsing rules for any existing native tool.

Both fields are required. `after_ordinal` is a JSON integer literal (no fraction or exponent), 0 through `2^53-1`; for zero, `after_hash` MUST be `-`. For nonzero, `after_hash` is canonical base64url SHA-256 of the exact locally ACKed complete record at that ordinal. Unknown fields, duplicate fields, null, wrong types, malformed hashes, or inconsistent zero/hash pair return `400 invalid_params`; this authenticated native rejection consumes its durably committed nonce. Unlike current `jack.*` v0.1 tools, this new candidate envelope is strict. Existing tools retain their current unknown-argument last-occurrence/inert-member rules unchanged.

On success the outer response uses the current native JSON-RPC shape with the original request ID. `current`, `records`, `head`, and `complete` MUST describe one immutable latest committed ring snapshot and the same response signing-time sample; do not mix fields from separate reads. A final emission guard checks exact ring revision, current tuple, and signer/key-interval eligibility. If changed, discard and return candidate unsigned `503 rotation_state_unavailable`; never re-sign, fallback, or retry. Client `current` must match the response signer and pre-response acknowledged state; it is never a trust root.

```json
{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":<request-id>,"result":{"content":[{"type":"text","text":"<JSON text of structuredContent>"}],"structuredContent":{"current":{"kid":"...","public_key":"..."},"records":["<canonical R_...>","..."],"head":{"ordinal":0,"hash":"-"},"complete":true},"isError":false}}
```

Before semantic parity checks, strictly parse the entire candidate response envelope against exact schemas: JSON-RPC version `2.0` and the original request ID; success `result` with required boolean `isError:false`, `content`, and `structuredContent`; exactly one content item with exactly `type` and `text`; and structured content with exactly `current`, `records`, `head`, and `complete`. Require exact types and reject extra or duplicate members at every candidate envelope, result, and nested object level, including `current` and `head`. The content item's text MUST be valid UTF-8 containing one strictly parsed JSON object. Deep-compare that object with `structuredContent`: object member order is irrelevant, array order is significant, strings compare as decoded exact strings, and integer-valued numbers compare by integer lexeme (not floating-point coercion). The head hash is exactly `-` only at ordinal zero, otherwise canonical unpadded base64url SHA-256. Validate strict public-key encodings and recompute every `kid` from its raw public key. Authenticate the complete outer response bytes before parsing the envelope; this does not introduce JSON canonicalization. The 256 KiB cap applies to the actual complete HTTP response body bytes, including the entire envelope and text/structured representations, not only the records or decoded result.

The result cursor `after_ordinal/after_hash` identifies the caller's acknowledged checkpoint, not authority. An optional exact retained duplicate prefix may precede the new contiguous extension. At most 16 total complete records are returned. A duplicate prefix must terminate exactly at the request cursor, then new links start at cursor+1; duplicate-only is a no-op. Let `terminal` be final delivered link or cursor when no links; `complete:true` requires head exactly equals terminal, `complete:false` requires `head.ordinal>terminal.ordinal`, and empty records are valid only for cursor=head and complete=true. An incomplete empty page is invalid, so caller pagination always advances.

### Errors and verification precedence

The existing outer verification precedence is unchanged: unknown outer server `kid` first, freshness second, signature third. After successful outer authentication, enforce the authenticated response `ts` assignment to its known key interval before parsing `current`, `head`, or records. Only then parse strict result shape, check binding/current consistency, and validate record links. A chain cannot authenticate its own response carrier. Signed native errors are handled before parsing rotation payloads; unsigned error bodies/headers are never trusted. An unknown outer signer is rejected before payload inspection even if its record would link to the client's chain. A known, ordinarily valid old signer can authenticate the carrier before a first-bridge expiration refusal.

After checking the authenticated outer response's timestamp assignment against the client's known key interval, run ClockGuard readiness before payload parsing/trust mutation. Then parse strict result/page shape (`rotation_result_invalid`), validate intrinsic exact record codec/key/proof/arithmetic (`rotation_record_invalid`), detect authenticated conflict at known position (`rotation_conflict`), reject missing/gapped/unlinked prefix or invalid head/completeness (`rotation_gap`), enforce first bridge `t<A_(m+1)` (`rotation_bridge_expired`), and perform the whole-trust callback (`rotation_state_changed` for stale/revision conflict; `rotation_state_unknown` for unknown callback outcome). A server-side candidate tool response distinguishes `400 invalid_params`, postcommit `409 rotation_checkpoint_unavailable`, and `503 rotation_state_unavailable`; after a committed request nonce, use ordinary signed native rejection only when a signer is eligible at final emission, otherwise unsigned 503. The client never trusts unsigned error bodies or retries automatically.

## 4. Client trust and record acceptance

`W` MUST be a positive whole-second integer, no greater than 120; 30 is the current v1 default. It is immutable per initialized rotation binding and is not inferred or changed by response/record. Existing `Profile.server_public_key`, profile imports, `Profile.kid` (the user's key ID), static credentials, and user-key rotation remain unchanged. A static client does not trust an announced key and does not perform TOFU.

### Durable client clock guard (independent of trust-extension callback)

Every rotation-enabled client MUST maintain a durable `ClockGuard` record in the same local transactional trust-store domain, separate from (not part of) the whole-trust-extension snapshot/CAS. It contains exact binding reference, immutable W, monotone whole-second H, monotone clock_revision, and a durable unsafe/fenced bit. Trust and ClockGuard updates use one enforced per-binding serialized store owner and atomic transaction boundary; there is no callback-only or in-memory substitute. Before accepting a response or processing an exact duplicate/extension, call a separate atomic ClockGuard operation; trust-extension callback counts exclude that operation. An independently trusted external monotonic-time service is not selected by this candidate. At a given protected-client verification, the first completed ClockGuard check provides an authoritative `t` for timestamp freshness, key interval, and content bridge; if cryptographic/payload processing takes time, the final ClockGuard check provides new `t_final`, and all checks including freshness/interval/strict cutoff/bridge must still pass at `t_final`. The final check is mandatory before response exposure or trust success. No check is rescued by an earlier time sample. If raw time is greater than H, that operation durably sets H to raw time and increments `clock_revision` exactly once; if equal, it returns the same state without increment; if below H or invalid/out of range, it durably latches unsafe and increments revision once before reporting `rotation_clock_unsafe`. It never clamps or clears the latch at catch-up. A definite failure or unknown write outcome closes the binding and returns `rotation_clock_unavailable`; no response success, key acceptance, or callback invocation is allowed. Never retry an unknown clock write.

The ClockGuard operation serializes its transaction in the same durable local trust-store domain. An ordinary verification or exact duplicate may durably advance H while invoking **zero trust-extension callbacks**. Callback counts below count only trust-extension CAS invocations, not ClockGuard operations. A trust callback takes exact `expected_trust`, `desired_trust`, and `expected_clock_tuple=(binding,W,H,clock_revision,fenced)`. It invokes the persistence callback at most once. The callback's storage adapter MUST perform one atomic compare-and-write transaction checking the full expected trust snapshot/revision and the current ClockGuard tuple, then write exactly the desired full trust snapshot/revision while leaving the ClockGuard record byte-for-byte unchanged. ClockGuard advances and trust CAS therefore serialize at the durable store's transaction boundary even though they are separate logical records. If ClockGuard changed before CAS, CAS is a definite conflict (one callback invocation), not a reason to retry or merge. If ClockGuard advances after a successful trust CAS, the final gate separately checks its latest state; it cannot undo either committed clock or trust state. A callback failure/unknown write outcome is not retried and fences as specified below.

On startup, load and validate both trust and ClockGuard for exact binding/W. Trust snapshot H must not exceed guard H, its clock revision must not exceed guard revision, and all durable fences are clear. Before traffic, sample raw clock and call ClockGuard.advanceAndCheck. If cutoff C was previously observed and the same authoritative guard survives, it has H>=C; restart at C-1 durably latches unsafe and MUST NOT revive old-key eligibility. Missing/corrupt/unsupported guard, unsafe bit, or failed/unknown guard write remains closed; never initialize an existing binding at zero. Local state cannot detect correlated rollback of both records by a self-consistent backup; require independent monotonic evidence or OOB/operator recovery. No backup rollback protection is claimed.



For a key `K_i` (`i>=1`), authenticated response timestamp MUST satisfy `ts>=A_i`; when successor `R_(i+1)` is known, it MUST also satisfy `ts<A_(i+1)`. K0 has no lower bound and no known upper bound until R1 is accepted. Candidate strict final client cutoff requires final raw ClockGuard sample `t<A_(i+1)` for a known successor; at equality the old key is rejected even if `ts` was earlier. Freshness remains separately inclusive: `abs(t-ts)<=W`. At `ts=A_i`, earliest fresh time is `A_i-W` inclusive if the interval is nonempty; `A_i-W-1` is stale. Equal activations may leave a validly authorized key with an empty response interval. If a successor is suppressed, the client cannot know the true cutoff.

For an ACKed prefix ending at ordinal `m`, accept exact stored duplicate records and/or a contiguous new extension. A response MAY begin with an optional contiguous duplicate prefix already ACKed by the client, followed by new records starting exactly at `m+1`; the prefix MUST be byte/hash-identical to the client's records and its terminal ordinal/hash MUST equal `m`. It cannot bridge a missing record or authorize a gap. If every returned record is duplicate, it is a no-op with zero trust-extension callbacks (ClockGuard still runs); it never moves P/A or clears a fence. Reject gaps, conflicting duplicates, altered links, wrong adjacent key, key reuse among retained current/pending/retired keys, wrong binding, bad proofs, decreasing `P_i`, or any `P_i` later than the authenticated outer response timestamp. Finite retained history cannot prove all-time non-reuse or a global single view; do not claim either. For each eligible nonduplicate extension, run ClockGuard and take a fresh pre-callback raw time `t`; require `t < A_(m+1)` before invoking the callback. If an exact whole-snapshot callback ACK arrives after A, the snapshot remains committed but the carrier response fails its fresh final verification gate; do not roll the ACK back or return a stale success. This allows a K0-pinned client to learn R1 and R2 before A1 and requires R2 authorization by K1.

Client errors are local, not HTTP statuses. After ordinary outer checks (`unknown_server_key`, freshness, `bad_signature`, authenticated timestamp interval) and a successful initial ClockGuard check, process candidate result in this order: strict response/MCP envelope, current/head/page rules (`rotation_result_invalid`); intrinsic record codec/key/proof/arithmetic (`rotation_record_invalid`); authenticated conflicting duplicate (`rotation_conflict`); missing/gapped/unlinkable prefix (`rotation_gap`); first bridge at or after A (`rotation_bridge_expired`); stale writer/snapshot or definite CAS conflict (`rotation_state_changed`); unknown trust callback outcome (`rotation_state_unknown`, fence); final ClockGuard rollback (`rotation_clock_unsafe` after durable latch) or unavailable/unknown write (`rotation_clock_unavailable`, fence). No automatic retry, auto-refresh, fallback, or trust rollback. These errors invoke zero trust-extension callbacks before eligible CAS; an eligible CAS callback is called exactly once and never retried.


First-bridge extension acceptance requires an `after_ordinal` cursor exactly matching the client's ACKed ordinal/hash (or a server-provided retained duplicate prefix that verifies to that cursor). Take a fresh pre-callback time through ClockGuard and require `t < A_(m+1)` before invoking the whole-snapshot callback. Later links in that contiguous batch do not each require `t<A_i`; nondecreasing P means the first bridge is the earliest of them. At `t>=A_(m+1)`, reject extension with zero trust-extension callbacks even if the outer response from a known key passed ordinary verification. If the callback is invoked while eligible and an exact ACK arrives late, retain the committed snapshot but fail the carrier's final gate without rollback. For strict cutoff, use a final raw sample from ClockGuard's authoritative `advanceAndCheck`; at equality or later, the old key is rejected. Do not use a response timestamp or prior sample to bypass it. Unknown outer key still fails first. A K1-ACKed client may learn R2 under K1 before A2, or from still-eligible K0 before A1 if not yet bridged. A newly trusted key never verifies a timestamp before its lower activation.

## 5. Whole-snapshot ACK, fencing, and recovery

The durable client trust snapshot has one strict version-1 schema: `format_version=1`; exact endpoint/audience binding; OOB anchor/checkpoint ordinal, hash, public key, and any lower-boundary state needed to verify the first retained successor; immutable `W`; ACKed head and exact retained complete `R` bytes/hashes; every admitted public key with its known lower bound and cutoff plus all pending records; bounded retired public-key records or a compact trusted checkpoint; trust revision and durable fences; and the copied ClockGuard tuple used as a CAS dependency, which is not time authority. Store no private key material or signer references in client trust state. A new binding can be initialized only from its OOB anchor/checkpoint; a nonzero checkpoint must be exact and independently provisioned, never taken from a remote response. The loader rejects missing/corrupt state, unsupported versions or fields, duplicate fields, malformed types, holes, hash/proof/key/schedule/binding mismatches, cap violations, and trailing data; it never repairs, truncates, merges, or initializes an existing binding from partial data.

A client trust-extension attempt uses a per-binding writer token. If token acquisition fails or the expected pre-callback snapshot is stale, reject before callback with zero trust-CAS invocations. Do not hold a mutex across cryptography, callback/storage I/O, or application code; serialize with token/CAS and reread state at final gates. Ordinary committed-snapshot reads may continue while the writer is pending; tentative keys never become trusted.

An eligible extension invokes the callback exactly once. The callback atomically compares the exact full expected trust snapshot/revision and captured ClockGuard tuple, then writes the complete desired trust snapshot with exactly one trust-revision increment, regardless of the number of new records. It MUST NOT change ClockGuard. Exact duplicates invoke zero trust-CAS callbacks and do not increment trust revision. An external trust or clock conflict is one definite noncommit, with no adoption, merge, or retry. A definite noncommit retains prior trust only while it remains eligible. A successful exact ACK installs the complete desired snapshot even if the carrier later fails its final freshness or cutoff gate; never roll it back.

Unknown callback outcome immediately fences the binding at every new-traffic and final gate. Persist the binding-wide fence; if its durability is uncertain, a pre-existing durable dirty marker or externally enforced startup gate MUST block every owner until explicit recovery. Under exclusive ownership, reconcile only the exact expected or exact desired complete snapshot and revision. Never adopt tentative state, repeat the callback, reset an epoch, or merge partial state. Readiness additionally requires exact configured/stored binding and W, a present and unfenced ClockGuard successful at a fresh sample, and every other state gate. Read-only reconciliation or matching W alone is insufficient. If the exact outcome cannot be established, remain fenced for OOB/operator recovery.

ClockGuard and trust state share the same local durable transaction store and enforced per-binding owner, but remain separate logical records. Only ClockGuard operations advance H/clock_revision or latch unsafe. A clock advance before the trust CAS causes one definite conflict; an advance after ACK is allowed, but the final gate rereads both records and fails closed if any condition changed. A self-consistent backup rollback of both records remains undetectable without independent monotonic evidence.

Before any final response success, ordinary client verification, duplicate result, or refresh result, use ClockGuard as described above and recheck inclusive freshness, authenticated response timestamp assignment, known interval and strict cutoff, exact binding, trust revision/state, and all fences. If the callback ACKed while eligible but fresh final gate fails due to cutoff or freshness, return local error without rollback. ClockGuard is the sole authority for monotonic client time; the trust callback's copied time/revision fields are validation dependencies only. A client has no private response signer; its readiness requires the OOB public-key anchor, trust state, and ClockGuard only. Server-side response-key and pending-tail signer availability is a separate server gate; absence fails the server closed without key fallback.

## 6. Server publication persistence and bounded history

Exactly one lifetime-exclusive server owner is permitted; a second opener is refused. Each publication uses an atomic transaction comparing the expected complete server state and revision, then writing the exact desired `P_i`, `R_i`, ordinal/hash and complete ring state, server-only protected opaque signer references, incremented revision, and a durable publication intent containing the exact expected/desired bytes. Signer references are opaque handles, not private-key material. After snapshot validation, sample `P_i` exactly once from a fresh trusted logical clock for authorization, immediately adjacent to validation of the exact current tail, next proof, and capacity; persist and authorize that fixed value without resampling or retry.

Only a confirmed durable atomic acknowledgment exposes the new publication. A definite abort leaves prior state unchanged and exposes nothing; a later attempt requires a new explicit publication authorization and is never an automatic retry. An unknown outcome fences publishing and signing at every new-work and final gate, exposes no tentative state, and is reconciled only against exact expected/desired state under the exclusive owner. If the durable fence cannot be confirmed, a dirty marker or external startup gate MUST block restart. No rollback, retry, auto-publication, or adoption of tentative state is permitted. Delay between acknowledgment and response does not alter `P_i`; `P_i` and `A_i` remain immutable. The publication transaction covers the rotation store only: it does not provide cross-file ACID with nonce state, signer custody, or handler effects.

The rotation server MUST maintain its own durable monotone ClockGuard/high-water and rollback latch in its rotation store, using the same `advanceAndCheck` semantics as the client: check fresh raw time and durably advance H before publication authorization, response-signer selection, or final emission; rollback/invalid time is durably latched and never cleared at catch-up. The server's dirty-serving epoch and unknown-publication fence must survive restart; startup under exclusive owner validates state/ClockGuard and drains/reconciles before service. A server clock uncertainty or unknown clock write fences publication and signing with no old-key fallback. This is separate from the nonce store's H and from client trust state. A self-consistent backup rollback is not detected locally.

On server restart, acquire the exclusive owner before opening, validate complete schema, binding, generation/revision, full chain hash/authorization/schedule, pending tail signer readiness, active interval signer availability, clock guard, and any durable dirty epoch/fence. Persist a dirty-serving marker before enabling publication or signed responses; clear it only after admission closes, all authorized response/signing/publication work drains, and all operations settle. If startup finds dirty/unknown publication state, reconcile exact expected/desired operation bytes under exclusive ownership; do not serve tentative state, auto-republish, or switch keys. Missing/corrupt/unsupported state, signer, clock rollback, unknown write, or unconfirmable cleanup fails closed. A prior response authorization cannot be physically revoked after emission. Backups do not provide rollback evidence.

Candidate engineering limits: 128 pending records, 1024 retained retired public records (with atomic checkpoint compaction), 16 records/page, 8192 decoded bytes/record, 256 KiB aggregate result body; the current transport's observed body cap is 1 MiB. These are engineering defaults, not owner-selected timers or guarantees. Always retain pending records, current signer key, and immediate boundary proof state. Compact only an eligible retired prefix into locally trusted checkpoint state using an atomic durable update; historical clients older than that checkpoint need OOB recovery. If no safe compaction is possible at capacity, refuse new publication before authorization/commit without evicting pending/current/boundary data. This cap is not a one-pending policy.

At response cutoff, disable old-key response signing even if its signer reference is retained for an in-flight authorization job. Keep a key reference until it is no longer needed for successor authorization, its response cutoff has passed, and authorized operations are quiescent; a same-second empty-interval key can still be needed to authorize its successor. A committed publication with delayed physical visibility keeps its original P/A, may fail closed or prevent late bridge, and is never extended to compensate.

## 7. Required future acceptance

Future implementation must provide independent Go, Rust, and TypeScript record/client references, matching all three conformance runners on the exact selected profile, and production-independent vectors. It must include MCP-native tool end-to-end behavior and security/backend acceptance on one exact source SHA. At minimum cover exact ten-/twelve-line bytes, full-record SHA-256, both proof messages, UTF-8 and canonical base64url, all field/range/arithmetic/key-admission errors; chain continuity, equal-time ordinal wins, empty intervals, arbitrary pending successors, P1 Monday10/P2 Monday11 timeline; exact current tuple and same signing ts; final emission and client cutoff boundaries; first bridge at A-1/A; suppressed suffix and unbounded unknown cutoff; page/checkpoint availability and head limitations; strict text/structuredContent parity and unknown argument rejection; whole-snapshot zero/one callback counts, definite conflict, exact ACK, unknown fence, late ACK, restart, stale W, missing/corrupt store, rollback latch; signer-tail authorization and no fallback; capacity compaction/refusal. No guessed crypto vectors or runtime guarantees are supplied by this document.

This candidate does not implement the tool or alter current native tools. Existing `jack.*` unknown-argument behavior remains unchanged.
