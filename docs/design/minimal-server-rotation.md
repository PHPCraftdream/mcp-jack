# Minimal server-key rotation: owner-directed candidate

**Status:** Task 52 design-only candidate; non-normative, unapproved, and unimplemented. It records the supplied D2/D3 direction without approving the codec, API, persistence contract, strict client cutoff, or other recommendations. Baseline named by the owner: `3398e7bde8aef1ae4d76ac55e482a06265fb5922`; supplied parent CI provenance is not evidence for this candidate. No commands, tests, builds, or runtime checks were run for this edit.

**Owner direction (English paraphrase, not verbatim):** “server can announce clients a new key any time; old key works another 48 hours after that announcement.” Owner additional statement: «каждый ключ живет48часов, нет ограничений на объявление нового ключа». These are owner statements; the additional statement is not an unconditional per-key TTL rule. Under the candidate schedule, OLD signs until the switch, then NEW signs. Thus if K1 is announced Monday 10 and K2 Monday 11, K0 switches to K1 Wednesday 10, and K1 expires when K2 takes over Wednesday 11; K1 has no independent TTL measured from its own announcement. “Any time” allows multiple pending announcements: no one-pending rule, cooldown, cancel, or reschedule is a product choice here. This does not approve unlimited pending storage; the engineering bound is in §6. D2 accepts a durable authenticated nonce persisted before handler dispatch; unknown write outcome fails closed and is never automatically retried. Neither direction approves this candidate's remaining choices.

## 2. Rotation chain, timer, and signing schedule

Let `K0` be the independently trusted initial server public key. Record `R_i` introduces `K_i`, for each ordinal `i >= 1`. `P_i` is the server's trusted logical-publication time in whole Unix seconds, nondecreasing by ordinal. Checked arithmetic defines `A_i = P_i + 172800`. Define `C_i = A_(i+1)`, the cutoff for `K_i` once its successor record is published. These are absolute times, never receipt-relative or sliding deadlines.

- `K0` may sign before `A1`; `K_i` may sign only in `[A_i, A_(i+1))` once the successor is known. For the latest published key, the upper bound remains unbounded until its successor is published. At exact equality the switch has happened: select the greatest ordinal among `0` and all `i` for which `A_i <= now`.
- `R_(i+1)` starts a new 48-hour period for `K_i`: `C_i=A_(i+1)`. A later `R_(i+2)` never moves that already-defined cutoff. OLD remains the response signer until the switch to NEW, not merely until the announcement is made. At cutoff, OLD is ineligible for response signing even if a private-key reference remains briefly for cleanup; after successor authorization commits, it has no further authorization role.
- The server selects the response signer at a whole-second signing-time sample from the latest committed ring; the authenticated `ts` and returned `current` tuple must describe that same key. After body buffering/signing, take a fresh final emission sample and revalidate the clock generation, fence, and latest committed ring; refuse/discard if the selected key is no longer eligible there. Never fallback, switch tuple, re-sign, or retry across a boundary. An ordinary read-only request may continue from committed state while a writer is pending; tentative state is never trusted. The final gate is a linearization check, not physical revocation of bytes already returned. A later `R_(i+2)` cannot extend an older key's fixed cutoff.
- Publication order is serialized, ordinal `i` strictly advances by one, and `P_i` cannot decrease. Equal `P_i` values are allowed. Same-second records have equal `A_i`; the strictly greater ordinal wins at equality, so any intermediate key with an empty `[A_i,A_(i+1))` interval cannot sign a response. Its chain authorization remains valid. Same-second announcements remain allowed; this candidate adds no delay, cooldown, or one-pending rule.
- No publication deadline is relative to a caller's receipt. A key's own announcement does not create an independent 48-hour key TTL: its response cutoff is determined by the next distinct announcement. There is no product-level pending-count restriction; §6's bound is an engineering capacity with compaction, not a one-pending policy.

**Concrete timeline:** with `P1=1000`, `P2=4600`, `A1=173800`, and `A2=177400`, K0 signs before 173800, K1 signs in `[173800,177400)`, and K2 signs at/after 177400. K1 expires at `P2+172800`, not `P1+172800`; a later `P3` does not extend K0's `A1` cutoff. In human terms, if R1 is announced Monday at 10 and R2 Monday at 11, K0 switches to K1 Wednesday at 10, and K1 expires Wednesday at 11 when K2 takes over.

## 3. Candidate record and delivery

### Canonical chain record

Bind each record to exact configured endpoint URL and audience (`aud`); no normalization, redirect, `Host`, response-header, or network-selected substitution. The URL must satisfy existing client URL policy. Candidate limits: URL 1–2048 UTF-8 bytes, audience 1–1024 UTF-8 bytes, decoded record at most 8192 bytes, canonical unpadded base64url. Reject invalid UTF-8/lone surrogates, alternate encodings, and oversize input before expensive work. Domain: `mcp-jack/server-key-rotation/min1`.

Let `B_i` be exactly ten ASCII lines joined by LF, without a trailing LF:

```text
mcp-jack/server-key-rotation/min1
<base64url(UTF8(exact_endpoint_url))>
<base64url(UTF8(exact_aud))>
<i>
<previous_complete_record_hash>
<old_kid>
<new_kid>
<new_public_key>
<P_i>
<A_i>
```

`i` is canonical decimal, strictly sequential and bounded by `2^53-1`; checked arithmetic rejects overflow rather than wrapping. For `R1` only, `previous_complete_record_hash` is the exact marker `-`, tied to the OOB initial pin at ordinal zero. Thereafter it is canonical base64url (43 characters) of SHA-256 over the complete previous canonical `R`. Each `old_kid`/`new_kid` is the canonical 43-character base64url SHA-256 key ID of the adjacent admitted 32-byte Ed25519 public key; `new_kid` is recomputed. `P_i` and `A_i` are canonical unsigned decimal whole seconds in the existing 12-digit timestamp domain; require `A_i` to equal checked `P_i+172800`. Same-second `P_i` is allowed; ordinal is the tie-breaker.

The two proofs are:

```text
proof_new = Ed25519_sign(new_private,
    ASCII(D + "/next-proof\n") || B_i)
old_authorization = Ed25519_sign(old_private,
    ASCII(D + "/authorize\n") || B_i || LF || ASCII(base64url(proof_new)))
R_i = B_i || LF || ASCII(base64url(proof_new)) || LF ||
    ASCII(base64url(old_authorization))
```

`D` is the domain above. Thus `R_i` is exactly twelve LF-separated lines, no trailing LF; proof and authorization are canonical base64url encodings of 64-byte signatures. The predecessor authorization binds the domain, ordinal, previous full-record hash, adjacent key IDs/public key, and both times. **All canonical bytes and wire details remain a candidate**, not an approved API or codec. Use existing key-admission/signature rules; do not import sr1's Ed25519 equation or assert new backend guarantees. No crypto outputs or SDK agreement are claimed.

A pending tail's private key may authorize its successor before it is eligible to sign responses. R2 must be authorized by K1, never by mistakenly reusing K0 to authorize R2. Authorization capability and response-signing eligibility are separate.

### Read-only refresh result

Recommend an explicit caller-invoked authenticated read-only `jack.server.key` tool. A response carries the response-time `current: {kid, public_key}`, a bounded contiguous list of complete records, and an authenticated head `{ordinal, hash}` plus a completeness indicator. The exact envelope/API bytes remain a candidate; it may reduce duplicated surfaces, but any retained text/`structuredContent` mirror must be strict-shape parsed on both surfaces and semantically match. The outer response signature authenticates its exact bytes; do not invent a second JSON canonicalization.

A refresh request may carry the client's accepted checkpoint ordinal/hash as a public delivery hint, never as authority. Return at most 16 contiguous links per refresh; the authenticated head identifies the server's chain head even if this reply is incomplete. A head claim alone is not a chain proof or completeness oracle. A complete result must link the client's checkpoint to that head. Never treat a missing link or untrusted head as permission to skip generations. Head and completeness metadata are scoped to this response; persist the accepted chain head, not a history of every advertised head. The response may include a bounded, exact retained prefix already known to the client before the new extension; the client checks those duplicates against its own retained hashes/bytes. A wrong or unavailable checkpoint hint fails closed and requires OOB recovery, not a guessed bridge. Incomplete pagination is caller-driven: each next page is a separate explicit request with its normal fresh authenticated nonce; the client does not auto-fetch or retry.

The existing outer verification order is exact: reject an unknown outer `kid` first; check freshness next; verify the outer signature next; only after successful authentication check the authenticated `ts` assignment against that key's known interval, then parse rotation content. A current/head tuple or delivery hint is never authority and cannot make an unknown outer key known. A complete chain cannot bootstrap its own carrier signature. Signed native errors are recognized before parsing link data; unsigned error bodies/headers never establish trust.

## 4. Client chain acceptance and response gates

An OOB initial checkpoint is K0, exact binding, positive immutable local freshness window `W`, and ordinal-zero chain checkpoint. The client retains its ACKed prefix and needed interval metadata, pending-tail keys/records, bounded retired public records/checkpoint, binding, `W`, clock history, head/revision, and fence state. `W` is local immutable policy: it is not in a record and cannot be adopted or reset by refresh. A nonzero initial ordinal is allowed only when independently trusted OOB metadata supplies its exact checkpoint tuple; it is not established by a remote head claim.

For an ACKed prefix ending at ordinal `m`, accept only an exact contiguous new extension `[m+1..n]`, optionally preceded by a bounded contiguous sequence of exact retained duplicates that matches locally stored hashes/bytes. A duplicate-only result is a no-op. Wrong, gapped, or conflicting duplicates fail closed; no pre-callback bridge-time test is needed for an exact duplicate, but it must match the client's own stored record. New links require increasing sequential ordinals, matching previous full-record hashes, adjacent old/new keys, valid next-key possession and predecessor authorizations, and no binding mismatch. Every `P_i` must be no later than the authenticated outer response `ts`; `P_i` must be nondecreasing. Take a fresh pre-callback time `t` and require `t < A_(m+1)` for the first new bridge. There is no individual expiry rejection for later links: nondecreasing `P_i` makes their activations no earlier than the first bridge. There is no fixed 48-hour allowance from receipt. For a nonzero prefix whose latest key has no known successor, its upper cutoff remains unknown; ordinary freshness and known lower-bound assignment still apply, and the bridge to its successor must independently pass the fresh `t < A_(m+1)` check.

Consequences that distinguish first-seen bridge from each link's own activation:

- A K0-pinned client can learn R1 and R2 while still before A1, including when the outer response is signed by K0; R2 still needs K1's authorization.
- A client ACKed through R1 can learn R2 under K1 before A2, or via an eligible K0 response before A1 if it has not yet crossed the R1 bridge. It does not have to wait for K1's response interval to start to authenticate R2.
- A first-seen bridge received at/after its A is refused with zero callbacks even if the outer signature is an ordinarily valid old-pin response. If the outer signer is an unknown successor, reject it as unknown before parsing. Neither case is TOFU; use OOB recovery.

For any known key, authenticated response `ts` must lie in its known interval: `ts >= A_i` for `K_i` (no lower bound for K0), and `ts < C_i` once its successor is known. The client final verification sample must be strictly before a known `C_i`; at/after cutoff, the old key is unknown even for a response signed before the cutoff. Freshness remains separately inclusive: `abs(t-ts) <= W`. Thus a newly ACKed K_i can first verify a timestamp `ts=A_i` at `t=A_i-W`, inclusive, provided its known interval is nonempty and final `t<C_i`; at `A_i-W-1` it is stale. If the successor has not been received, the client cannot know the true upper bound: suppression of that successor can delay cutoff knowledge. Do not fabricate a global known expiry. After a valid extension, all final gates use its updated cutoff and reject an old response if the cutoff was crossed. This strict final cutoff is a proposed availability trade-off and remains an owner review gate.

Initial K0 has no known upper cutoff until R1 is learned. A first valid K0-carried extension is authenticated ordinarily before record parsing, then must pass the fresh `t<A1` bridge check. A K0 response delivered after that newly learned A1 authenticates at the ordinary layer but the extension fails without a callback or trust mutation. After R1 is ACKed, retained interval metadata prevents later revival of K0. A valid extension makes the new key known, but a signature cannot use it before its lower activation time.

For ordinary responses and refresh results, apply the existing outer verification order: unknown `kid`, freshness, signature, then authenticated `ts` assignment to the key's known interval. For a refresh result, `current` must match the response signer by exact admitted `kid` and public-key bytes and be consistent with that signer's schedule in the client's currently known snapshot; it is consistency data, never a trust root. An incomplete result does not prove the server chain complete: the client does not infer missing links or global cutoff knowledge from the head, and a current signer not already represented/eligible in its pre-response snapshot cannot be bootstrapped from the result. A binding mismatch, interval/assignment violation, or tuple mismatch rejects before callback. Recheck freshness, assignment, updated cutoff, and key eligibility at every applicable final gate. Signed native errors are handled before attempting to parse any rotation result.

An exact retained-record duplicate is a no-op: no callback, no deadline reset, no null-based trust clearing. It is acceptable only on the exact ACKed chain and does not restore expired-key response eligibility. Conflicting valid records at a known ordinal/hash position are rejected and may latch a local binding fence pending OOB resolution; invalid proof does not fence. This is local hash-conflict detection, not a global fork service or claim of deployment-wide single view. Reject reuse of current, pending, or retained retired keys. Do not forbid the current tail's required predecessor-authorizer role. Finite history cannot prove all-time non-reuse: prohibit reuse against retained history only, and make no all-time promise. R1's `-` predecessor marker is valid only against the independently trusted K0 anchor; it cannot be replayed against a reused retained key or checkpoint.

If an owner demands that every key ever announced have a nonzero response interval, equal activation times expose a contradiction that state-only monotonic times and serialized ordinal do not prevent. The chosen candidate permits an empty intermediate interval; do not silently add a delay, cooldown, or “one pending” rule.

## 5. Persistence, concurrency, and recovery

The candidate ACK snapshot is the **entire client trust state** for the exact binding and configured/stored `W`: OOB checkpoint, acknowledged head and contiguous retained records/hashes, pending ring, bounded interval/checkpoint metadata, admitted public keys, retired public history, clock/fence/revision. Client ACK snapshots contain no private keys or server signer references. Server publication uses a separate server-only snapshot/bucket for exact `P_i`, `R_i`, pending ring, signer references, and revision; server signer references are never required in client persistence. Use a per-binding writer token; do not hold locks across I/O, cryptography, or callbacks. Prepare a valid extension, then freshly recheck authorizer, binding, state/revision, `W`, key assignment, freshness, and bridge time before one atomic callback for the whole extension. No tentative key becomes trusted before exact ACK.

An overlapping request rejected as stale against its pre-callback snapshot, or unable to acquire the writer token, is rejected before callback (zero callback invocations). Exact duplicates are checked against the client's own stored hashes/bytes and have zero callbacks. The CAS exists only inside the callback: once invoked, a backend may invoke it once and report an external concurrent revision conflict; treat that as not committed, do not adopt tentative state, and never retry. A definite non-commit retains prior state only if it is still eligible. Exact ACK installs the exact desired state; a late ACK may commit the full ring but its carrier can fail the fresh final gate, with no rollback. Unknown outcome fences the binding at traffic/final gates until authoritative reload of the exact expected/desired state; never retry, guess rollback, adopt tentative keys, or fallback. Recheck state, binding/`W`, fence, clock, signer readiness, outer-key eligibility, freshness, and assignment at all applicable content/final gates.

ACK outcomes are definite: committed exact ACK installs and may use only the exact binding/`W`/record revision; definitely not committed retains prior state only if eligible; unknown immediately fences the affected binding at all new-traffic and final gates until authoritative reload of the exact expected/desired state, with no rollback, retry, or key fallback. Do not require cryptographic verification under a mutex; the writer token provides serialization only. **Read-only reconciliation of the same stored `W` alone cannot restore readiness: restore the exact matching configured `W` and satisfy all other state, fence, clock, and signer-readiness gates before normal operation can resume, without epoch resets.**

A valid committed snapshot can serve ordinary read-only responses while a refresh writer is pending. A pending/unknown write outcome cannot make tentative keys trusted. A gate orders authorization, not actual physical return time: an earlier authorized response is not revoked merely because a later fence is installed.

Live serialized clock samples cannot roll back: detect unsafe rollback, latch closed, and require explicit recovery. Across restart, preserve trusted chain/history and require a trusted startup clock gate; backups are not a rollback oracle, and no NTP or all-history-loss protection is claimed. Missing/corrupt initialized client state is not a new pin. An immutable binding or `W` mismatch is not adoption. Never revive OLD from installed-next/retired state.

## 6. Bounded history and publication lifecycle

These are explicit engineering capacity bounds, not product timer/pending restrictions: at most 128 pending records, 1024 retained retired public records, 16 links per refresh, 8192 decoded bytes per record, and 256 KiB aggregate refresh body (below the observed existing 1 MiB response buffer cap). A pending record is one with `A_i > t` at the trusted time `t`; a same-second intermediate key with a known empty response interval becomes retired at `t >= A_i`. Retire a record only after trusted time reaches its known response cutoff and the locally ACKed current floor has advanced beyond it. Always retain every pending record and the current record plus its immediate boundary record(s). Compact the oldest eligible retired prefix into a locally trusted checkpoint tuple (ordinal, full-record hash, admitted key, predecessor interval, and required state) so the retained suffix remains chain-contiguous from that checkpoint. Make checkpoint creation and pruning one atomic durable update; only after it is durable may oldest retired records beyond the 1024-record window be trimmed. Retired-history fullness therefore triggers compaction, not permanent append refusal; if no eligible prefix can yet be compacted, refuse publication before exposing a new record without evicting pending/current/boundary state. Historical clients older than the checkpoint require OOB recovery: never send a checkpoint as remote authority or as a substitute for verifiable links. A new client may start at a nonzero ordinal only from independently trusted OOB checkpoint metadata. R1's `-` marker cannot be replayed against the same reused K0 anchor; reject retained-key reuse. Backups are outside rollback protection. These finite checks do not provide an all-time identity blacklist or non-reuse guarantee.

Serialize server publication append/CAS of exact `P_i`, `R_i`, pending ring, server-only signer references, and revision; expose only after exact durable ACK. Exact replay/clone duplicates are no-ops and cannot change a fixed `P_i`. Startup validates binding, chain arithmetic, keyring, clock, required active signer, every pending/tail authorization signer, and immutable metadata before service. Missing required signer or unsafe clock fails closed without fallback. Publication may use a pending tail's private key to authorize its successor before that key becomes response-active. Keep each old private signer reference until its successor authorization is committed, its response cutoff has passed, and signing/authorization jobs are quiescent; at the cutoff disable response signing even if cleanup still holds a reference. After successor authorization commits, no new authorization needs the old key. Same-second empty-interval keys may still need to authorize their successor. Retain bounded public proof/history only; never retain private keys merely for proof validation. Secure erasure and backup behavior are not claimed.

`P_i` is proposed here as a trusted logical publication-authorization sample; that definition is unapproved and is not a measured first-visibility time. Serialized durable publication and physical exposure are not one atomic instant. A delayed ACK at/after `A_i` keeps exact `P_i/A_i`, cannot reset/cancel/extend the timer, and may leave service fenced or clients unable to bridge; require explicit recovery rather than an invented new start time. **The actual definition/guarantee of publication visibility is a genuine owner ambiguity if the contract requires 48 hours from first physical visibility.** This candidate does not claim that guarantee; resolve it before implementation if required.

## 7. Delivery, D2, compromise, and backend boundary

Refresh is explicit caller-invoked only: no unsolicited push, automatic polling, background refresh, auto-pagination, retry, profile mutation, or automatic compromise fix. Suppressed delivery leaves old-pin-only clients unable to authenticate a new outer signer; use OOB recovery. A compromised currently authorized predecessor can authorize malicious successors; chaining does not repair that compromise. Exact ordinal/hash links provide local continuity, not deployment/server identity stabilization or global fork prevention.

D2's authenticated nonce must be durable before handler dispatch; an unknown write outcome fails closed and is never automatically retried. This is distinct from rotation publication/ACK and is not implemented by observed `server/nonce.go`. See the companion [restart/replay design](restart-replay.md). Backend approval and new-library research are separate work; cross-reference the separate [durable nonce backend design](durable-nonce-backend.md), without implying its approval or any library/security guarantee here. TLS and current endpoint policy remain necessary. Never parse unsigned error bodies or headers for trust.

## 8. Candidate discrimination vectors (not generated or run)

- **Chain bytes:** exact ten-line B/twelve-line R; first predecessor marker `-`; subsequent SHA-256 full-R hash; changed binding/ordinal/hash/adjacent key/P/A/signature, alternate base64url, malformed UTF-8, bad key admission, oversize, and `P_i > outer ts` reject with zero callback. No fabricated crypto vectors.
- **Arithmetic/ties:** `P1=1000`, `A1=173800`; `P2=4600`, `A2=177400`. Reject altered `A != checked(P+172800)`, overflow, 12-digit overflow, ordinal wrap, and decreasing P. `P1=P2=1000` gives equal activation; R1/R2 authorization remains valid, K1 response interval is empty, and K2 is selected at the tie. No callback retry or interval fabrication.
- **Every boundary:** for each known cutoff C, test old `ts=C-1` at final `t=C-1` versus `t=C`/`C+1`; test new `ts=A`, `t=A-W-1`, `A-W`, and `A-W+1`; test strict `ts<C` and lower-bound assignment. Refresh receipt never moves A/C. Verify K1's cutoff is `P2+172800`, not `P1+172800`, and later R3 does not move K0's C.
- **Bridge/signer:** first-seen extension at `t=A_bridge-1` versus `A_bridge`; known old outer response ordinarily verifies before an expired-bridge refusal with zero callback; unknown outer signer fails before parse. Prefix R1 may receive R2 from K1 before A2, or from K0 before A1 if still eligible. For an unseen successor, do not claim the client knows the global upper cutoff.
- **Delivery/head:** complete contiguous prefix; partial links plus authenticated head/completeness; missing/pruned prefix, untrusted head, conflicting valid suffix, exact duplicate, key reuse, mismatched mirror/shape, and more than 16 links. Check current against the pre-response known snapshot and outer signer; a head beyond delivered links is not chain completeness. No TOFU or self-authenticating head.
- **Concurrency/fences:** whole-snapshot CAS; stale pre-callback snapshot/busy-token rejection (zero callbacks), backend-invoked CAS revision conflict (one callback, no adoption/retry), unknown callback result, late ACK, stale `W`, configured `W=30` versus stored `W=30` with runtime config changed to `60`, unknown outer key, invalid proof, binding mismatch, and a final gate crossing C. The W mismatch invokes zero callbacks and does not adopt `60`; read-only reconciliation of the stored `30` alone does not restore readiness. Unknown fences do not rollback an ACKed ring; no tentative trust, fallback, rollback, or automatic retry. Matching configured W and all state/fence/clock/signer gates are required, with no epoch reset.
- **Capacity/publication:** 128 pending plus one more refuses before publication without eviction/cooldown; same-second empty-interval records retire at activation; full retired window compacts an eligible retired prefix atomically before trimming beyond 1024, while an ineligible full window refuses before exposure; record and aggregate-body limits; delayed ACK at/after A preserves exact deadline; exact replay/clone does not change P. Historical clients older than the checkpoint require OOB recovery; full-history reuse is not claimed.
- **Availability:** ordinary committed-state reads can continue while a writer is pending; a late carrier may fail after exact ACK without rollback. Gates are linearization checks, not physical response revocation. Suppressed delivery, restart, missing signer, rollback clock, and OOB recovery all fail as stated.
